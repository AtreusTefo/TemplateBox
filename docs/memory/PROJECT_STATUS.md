## What TemplateBox Is

A 100% client-side, serverless template personalization engine at templatebox.win. Four live tools, backed by nine search-intent landing pages, a guides blog, and About/Terms/Privacy pages. The tools: a CV/Resume builder, a Business Document builder (rent receipts, cash payment receipts, itemized business receipts, sales and cash receipt forms, invoices, employee warning notices), a Poster/Canvas creator, and a Product Mockup generator for print-on-demand sellers (t-shirt, hoodie, mug, packaging box). Monetized through a mandatory 10-second ad loading page between the catalog and the editor. Full requirements and design system live in `PRD.md` and `CLAUDE.md`.

**The homepage rail IS the editor rail, sharing one CSS rule (August 9, 2026).** Asked for in those words after several intermediate shapes were tried and rejected — a height-tiered creative swap, then a `position: fixed` full-height column at the window edge with `body` padding reserving its width. **The fixed full-height column was reinstated on August 10, 2026 and is now the shipping shape** (see the decision-log entry for that date); what stayed dead from those experiments is the height-tiered creative swap and any second standalone `.home-rail` block, because a survivor would fight the shared rule silently. What stands:

- **`.editor-rail, .home-rail { ... }` is one selector list, not two similar blocks.** That is the only reading of "the same rail" that cannot drift apart later. Everything comes from there: `position: fixed` from `top: 0` to `bottom: 0` at `right: 0`, the width token, the column stacking and the internal scroll. The accepted trade is that on a short viewport the lower slots sit inside a scroller most visitors will not scroll — the same trade the editors already make.
- **Both of the editors' bands are kept: the 300x250 stack at 93rem and up, a single 160x600 from 75rem to 93rem.** Only the floor differs (75rem, not the editors' 84rem), because below 84rem an editor's rail comes out of its fixed panes while the feed's masonry columns just reflow. **The 160x600 band was dropped once and had to be put straight back.** The arithmetic said it was unnecessary — at 1200px a 300px rail still leaves three columns of roughly 257px, wider than the 185px the phone layout ships — and the arithmetic was beside the point. Reported from a 1366px laptop as "this is not a side rail": a 300px unit at that width is as wide as a content column, so it stops reading as a rail beside the feed and reads as a fourth column of adverts. `tests/verify-layout.js` now asserts the mounted unit is narrower than one feed column at every width in the band. **Width that is affordable is not the same as width that looks right**, and no amount of column-width arithmetic will tell you which you have.
- **The homepage `display: none` gate is declared after the shared rule, deliberately.** Media queries carry no specificity. Written up in the homepage section — where it looks like it belongs — it would sit above the shared `display: flex` at identical specificity and lose, leaving the rail visible on every viewport it is meant to skip. `tests/verify-layout.js` asserts the source order.
| Banner 320x50 (reused zone) | **`index.html`**, fixed anchor at the foot of the viewport, under 48rem. Mutually exclusive with the rail above — the homepage never shows both, and shows nothing between 48rem and 75rem | `101fe70128e51351589ecd23ab2d0e21` |

Adult ads are toggled off for this site in the Adsterra dashboard.

**`index.html` carried zero ads from launch until August 6, 2026, and this document described that as non-negotiable.** It was reversed by the owner when the catalogue moved to a three-column masonry layout, which leaves real empty margin beside the content on a wide screen. What changed is the placement, not the reasoning: the original objection was that ad clutter on the indexed first-impression page carries SEO and trust risk, and a passive banner in the page's own margin — one that cannot navigate the tab, cannot cover content, and renders nothing at all below 75rem — is a different proposition from the Pop-Under that was removed the same day. **Active formats on `index.html` remain banned.** If homepage bounce rate or Search Console impressions move against you after this ships, the rail is one attribute to delete.

The editors carried none either until July 27, 2026, when that half of the rule was reversed on a dwell-time argument; they now carry passive banners selected by viewport band, up to three on very wide screens. The editors also carry no site footer — it was removed as unnecessary chrome on a workspace page, which means **the privacy policy and terms are not linked from any editor**; worth revisiting if that ever needs to be reachable from where visitors type personal data. See the decision log below and `docs/implementation/EDITOR_PAGE_AD_PLACEMENT.md`.

**`loading.html` also lost its footer and gained the site-wide anchor (August 2026).** The footer served no purpose on a ten-second, noindex intermediary — there is nowhere the links could send a visitor that outperforms waiting out the countdown. The anchor is a reversal of a documented exclusion in `js/ads.js`: it was originally left off specifically because a unit fixed to the foot of the viewport during a ten-second countdown reads as if it might cover the thing the visitor is waiting on. It was added back on the reasoning that `.has-site-anchor` reserves body padding *only once a banner has actually filled*, which is the same mechanism that already makes this placement safe on every other page carrying it — the anchor takes its place below the loading card, not over it. Verify this specifically on a real short viewport before trusting it; it has not been checked in a browser. The page's existing two 300x250 banners and the Social Bar are untouched; the anchor draws from `leaderboard`/`leaderboardMobile`, distinct zones from the inline banners' `inContent`/`endOfArticle` keys, so nothing on the page repeats a zone. One unverified interaction worth knowing about: the Social Bar is a self-injecting widget that positions itself independently per Adsterra's own instructions (see `SOCIAL_BAR_NOT_DISPLAYING.md`), and if it renders at the same screen edge as the fixed anchor the two could visually collide — low probability, since that document already establishes the Social Bar rarely displays at all on this page's short lifetime, but worth a look if both are ever seen firing together.

## The Monetized Flow (as implemented)

```
index.html card click
  -> js/app.js launchTemplate(): direct navigation to loading.html?target=...
     A modified click (ctrl/cmd/shift/alt/middle) opens that same interstitial
     in a NEW tab instead; the anchor's href points at the editor so crawlers
     see a real link, and the handler is what keeps human clicks monetized
  -> loading.html: 10s countdown, 2 banners + Social Bar render
  -> at 0: navigation watchdog re-issues location.replace(editorUrl) every 700ms
     until the page actually unloads, so ad-initiated navigations can't strand the user
  -> resume.html or poster.html: localStorage-backed editor carrying passive
     banners by viewport band (three 300x250 in the fixed rail at 93rem+, one
     160x600 in that same rail 84-93rem, 728x90 leaderboard 48-84rem, 320x50
     anchor below 48rem) and no active format. Editors carry no site footer
```

index.html's own click behaviours (the category tabs, the continue-strip discard button) are bound as window-capture delegated listeners in `js/app.js` rather than element-level listeners. This was originally forced: the click shield stopped non-launch clicks propagating below window, so an element listener would never have fired. The shield went with the Pop-Under on August 6, 2026 and the constraint no longer applies, but the pattern is still correct and was left alone. Background: `docs/error-fixes/POPUNDER_HIJACKS_ALL_PAGE_CLICKS_FIX.md`.

Those handlers key on the `data-filter` **attribute**, not on a wrapper class. Renaming the tab container `.filter-pills` to `.feed-tabs` during the August 9 rebuild silently killed category filtering, because three selectors in `js/app.js` still demanded the old class and nothing failed loudly. `tests/verify-layout.js` now reads every page and both scripts off disk and asserts that every hook a selector requires — class, id or `data-` attribute — actually exists in the markup. Note the correction: the filter handlers key on the `data-filter` **attribute**, so a check that scanned only class names would have sailed straight past this bug. It was written that way first and caught nothing until the mutation test exposed it.

`EDITOR_ROUTES` in `js/app.js` is the whitelist mapping `target` query values to editor pages — this is what makes the redirect immune to open-redirect tampering. `loading.html` carries a second, dependency-free copy of this same whitelist inline (see below) — **both must be updated when adding a new editor**, or the fallback path silently sends unrecognized/failed cases to the default editor instead of the new one.

An editor that serves several catalog cards (docs.html serves six) also needs a **variant hand-off**: `initCatalog()` writes the clicked card's `data-doc` value to `localStorage` under `tb_editor_preset`, and `TB.takePreset()` reads and clears it on arrival. The preset is never a route — the editor matches it against its own variant table, so a tampered value can only resolve to something that already ships. Clearing on read is what stops a later direct visit from re-applying a stale card choice over the visitor's saved work.

## Known Issues Already Solved (see docs/error-fixes/ for full write-ups)

- **R2_CUSTOM_DOMAIN_ON_APEX_TAKES_DOWN_SITE.md** (July 30, 2026) — the entire site returned 404 from Cloudflare, with no Netlify headers, rendering a storage "Object not found / Is this your bucket?" page while `templatebox.netlify.app` served 200. Cause: an R2 bucket (`product-mockups`) had `templatebox.win` — the apex, i.e. the website — bound as its Custom Domain. An R2 custom domain binds the *whole hostname* to the bucket, so nothing reached Netlify, and the bucket was empty. Uploading objects would not have helped: a bucket has no page routing and Cloudflare does not serve bucket listings at a domain root, so `/` 404s by design. **The remedy has a destructive second step:** removing the binding also deletes the DNS record it created, taking the site from 404 to NXDOMAIN, which reads as a worse failure — the apex `CNAME` to `templatebox.netlify.app` must then be recreated by hand, left DNS-only until Netlify verifies it, and only then set to Proxied. `www` also appeared broken throughout despite an intact record, because Netlify 301s it to the primary domain and the apex was what was failing. Resolved the same day; verified with `Server: cloudflare` and `x-nf-request-id` present on the same response.

- **RESUME_PDF_RASTERIZED_TEXT_FIX.md** — html2pdf.js rasterizes text (fails ATS parsing); replaced with jsPDF native `doc.text()` API. Verified with an automated PDF-stream inspection (real `Tj` text operators, no embedded images) plus a manual browser highlight-copy-paste test.
- **ADSTERRA_AD_CONFLICT_FIX.md** — Popunder's fallback redirect could hijack the foreground tab when popups were blocked (last navigation assignment wins); fixed with the 150ms deferred nav in `launchTemplate()`. Also: two banner tags sharing one page context clobbered each other's global `atOptions`; fixed by isolating each in its own `srcdoc` iframe. The deferral half of this fix was superseded July 27 (next entry); the srcdoc isolation stands.
- **POPUNDER_HIJACKS_ALL_PAGE_CLICKS_FIX.md** (July 27, 2026) — the hijack above was never launch-click-specific: the Popunder's document-level handler observes every click on index.html, so popup-blocked visitors were redirected off-site from filter pills (no competing navigation at all), nav links (coin-flip race) and even launch clicks (one-shot 150ms deferral loses to a fast-committing ad). Fixed with an inline window-capture click shield in index.html that starves the ad script of every non-`[data-target]` interaction and refuses injected foreign-origin anchors; pills and the continue-strip discard moved to window-capture delegation to survive the shield; `launchTemplate()` now re-issues its navigation every 700ms until unload (loading.html's watchdog pattern). Verified with a 16-check Playwright run against a simulated hijacking ad handler (attached earlier than the real one can be) with the ad redirect delayed 600ms to mirror a cross-origin round trip. Known cost: Clarity's document-level click capture on index.html is starved too; scroll/movement replay unaffected. Revised July 28, 2026: the shield alone bricked the page against the real ad script, which captures the first click with a transparent full-viewport layer (random id, z-index 2147483650) removed only from inside its own document-level listener -- the very listener the shield starves -- so no click or hover reached the page at all. An overlay neutralizer in the same inline block now forces pointer-events:none on any oversized fixed/absolute element the page does not ship (MutationObserver plus 700ms sweep, geometry-based because the id is random). Verified by reproducing the dead page with a faithful simulation of the decoded layer mechanism, then passing 9 overlay checks and the updated 17-check regression suite.
- **LOADING_REDIRECT_STALL_FIX.md** — same "last navigation wins" defect class, but spanning the whole loading.html page lifetime instead of one click; fixed with the persistent navigation watchdog described above.
- **SOCIAL_BAR_NOT_DISPLAYING.md** — not a defect. Full delivery chain verified (script serves, runtime domains resolve and respond). Non-display was per-visitor frequency capping plus the widget's animated entrance losing the race against the page's 10-second lifetime. Confirmed serving via Adsterra dashboard impression counts.
- **INTERNAL_FILES_PUBLICLY_SERVED.md** — `publish = "."` plus no build step meant the whole workspace was the web root: `docs/**` (including this file, with the Adsterra zone keys and Clarity ID), `PRD.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `GEMINI.md`, `README.md` and `.github/` all answered 200 and were crawlable. First fixed with `force = true` 404 redirect rules, then superseded the same day by moving the deployable site into `site/` and setting `publish = "site"`, so internal files are not in the deploy at all. Treat the previously-served contents as having been public.
- **STATIC_POST_PAGES_SERVED_NO_ADS.md** — the July 26 move to static post pages produced a generator shell with no ad hosts and no `js/blog.js` include, so four live Adsterra zones (leaderboard, in-content 300x250, end-of-article 300x250, 160x600 rail) served nothing on the pages readers actually reach; `post.html`, which did have them, had been 301'd away. Fixed by emitting the hosts in the generated markup and auto-filling them from the shared registry (`initStaticPost()` at first, replaced hours later by the generic `TBAds` auto-mount when the landing pages needed the same thing). Same generator and same class of omission as the footer drift below. ## Operational Knowledge Not Written Down Elsewhere

These cost real time to work out during setup and aren't captured in any other doc — worth reading before touching DNS, Search Console, or Adsterra again.

- **Cloudflare DNS + Netlify domain cutover order matters.** Add the custom domain in Netlify first; if Netlify shows "Netlify DNS propagating" it means it tried to create its own DNS zone — go to the Netlify team's Domains page and delete that DNS zone, then re-add the domain so it shows "Pending DNS verification" instead (external-DNS mode). Only then add the Cloudflare CNAME records.
- **SSL cert sequencing:** keep new DNS records gray-cloud (DNS only) until Netlify's Let's Encrypt certificate shows as issued in Domain management, only then flip to orange-cloud proxied. Proxying before the cert issues can block Netlify's verification. After flipping to proxied, set Cloudflare SSL/TLS to **Full (strict)** — leaving it on "Flexible" causes an infinite redirect loop against Netlify's forced HTTPS.
- **Google Search Console Domain properties want the bare hostname** (`templatebox.win`, no protocol) when creating the property, but want the **full absolute URL** (`https://templatebox.win/sitemap.xml`) when submitting a sitemap — the relative form (`sitemap.xml`) throws "Invalid sitemap address" on a Domain property because the host/protocol is ambiguous.
- **A freshly submitted sitemap shows "Couldn't fetch" instantly**, before Google has attempted any fetch — this is a UI placeholder, not a real failure. Don't chase it via Cloudflare Security Events unless the URL Inspection "Live Test" also fails; if Live Test passes, the sitemap status is cosmetic and will resolve on Google's own schedule (can take 1-2+ days). Forcing a re-check: delete and resubmit, or submit with a cache-busting query string (`sitemap.xml?v=2`) to force a treat-as-new fetch.
- **Cloudflare Security → Events** is the authoritative way to check whether Googlebot (or any specific requester) is being challenged/blocked — filter by path and/or user agent. A `curl` spoofing Googlebot's user-agent from a non-Google IP does NOT reproduce what Cloudflare's bot detection sees, since it checks IP/ASN and reverse DNS, not just the UA header — don't trust that kind of test as conclusive.
- **Adsterra's "Create Ad Unit" dialog only allows one placement per format-and-size per site** — it greys out exact duplicates. A second zone of a size you already have (e.g., a second distinct 300x250 Banner) requires a direct support ticket; be explicit in the ticket that you want a second zone for an *existing* ad slot's separate tracking, not additional ad inventory, or support may push back citing ad-density/CPM concerns. Different Banner sizes are NOT blocked, though: the 728x90, 320x50, and 160x600 blog zones were all created straight from the dashboard on July 18, 2026 with no ticket.
- **Adsterra's "Adult ads" toggle can reset to ON** on ad-unit-creation dialogs even after being turned off elsewhere for the site — recheck it each time before creating a new placement.
- **Ad script page resources always show as "couldn't be loaded" in Google's URL Inspection tool.** This is universal for any ad-monetized site (ad networks block crawler fetches of their own scripts) and is not a problem to fix.
- **Never bind an R2 bucket (or any Cloudflare product) to `templatebox.win` or `www`.** A custom domain binding takes over the entire hostname, so the website disappears — this took the site down on July 30, 2026. Asset hosting belongs on its own subdomain, e.g. `cdn.templatebox.win`. See `R2_CUSTOM_DOMAIN_ON_APEX_TAKES_DOWN_SITE.md`.
- **Verify domain routing by the header pair, never by the status code.** `Server: cloudflare` proves the proxy is in front; `x-nf-request-id` on the *same* response proves the request reached Netlify behind it. Either alone proves nothing — a 404 carrying `x-nf-request-id` is the site's own 404 page and healthy, whereas a Cloudflare-only response means the request never left Cloudflare. A bare 200 is not proof either, because R2 also returns 200 once objects exist.
- **Negative DNS caching outlasts a DNS fix, and local flushes do not reach upstream resolvers.** After the July 30 fix was correct at Cloudflare's authoritative nameservers and at 1.1.1.1/8.8.8.8/9.9.9.9, the local resolver kept returning NXDOMAIN and `Clear-DnsClientCache` did not help. Bypass DNS to test: `curl -D - --resolve templatebox.win:443:<edge-ip> https://templatebox.win/`. Treat a browser still showing an error as unproven, not as evidence the fix failed.
- **Netlify's "Pending DNS verification" plus a proxied record is a known deadlock.** Netlify resolves the hostname and sees Cloudflare's IPs instead of its own load balancer. Add or restore records grey-cloud (DNS only), wait for Netlify to verify and for the Let's Encrypt certificate to list the hostname, and only then switch to Proxied — then confirm SSL/TLS is Full (strict), because Flexible causes an infinite redirect loop against Netlify's forced HTTPS.
- **Two ignorable Cloudflare UI artifacts.** The DNS "Recommendations" panel ("Visitors cannot reach templatebox.win — add an A, AAAA or CNAME record") is cached and persists after the record exists; confirm against the record list instead. The apex-row tooltip about CNAME flattening is Cloudflare explaining that it makes an apex CNAME possible, not a warning.

## Design/Architecture Decisions Made During Build (with reasoning, in case revisited)

- **The ad rail is a fixed full-height column and the page is inset to its left (August 10, 2026).** `position: fixed; top: 0; right: 0; bottom: 0` at the band's width, on `index.html` and all four editors. The column owns the window's right edge from y=0 to the foot of the viewport; the menu bar ends where the column begins instead of running underneath it. This is the Photopea layout, asked for by name. **It reverses two things this document said the day before**: that the rail is sticky at `top: 5.75rem` deliberately clearing the header, and that a fixed full-height column with body padding "was tried and reverted". The owner reinstated it knowingly, to have the unit in full display, and asked for this entry so the next session does not revert it a second time. **Do not restore the sticky version.** Full write-up with every measurement: `docs/implementation/FIXED_FULL_HEIGHT_AD_RAIL.md`. The parts most likely to be second-guessed:
  - **The inset is ONE mechanism: a single `padding-right` on `body`**, applied by `.has-ad-rail`, which `js/ads.js` adds only once `mountPlacement` has actually filled a banner. Everything in normal flow inherits it, which is the entire reason `.site-header` needs no rule of its own -- it is `position: sticky`, so it stays in flow and its background stops cleanly at the column's edge. The mega-menu, the sticky category tabs and the editors' sticky export bar are all inside that padding for the same reason. **If a width or margin hack on the header ever looks necessary, the padding is in the wrong place.** The two `position: fixed` anchors are the only things that do not inherit it, and they mount only under 48rem where no rail exists -- checked, not assumed.
  - **The page width caps had to shed the reserved width, not reserve it twice.** `main:has(.editor-shell)` was raised to 84rem/93rem and `main:has(.home-layout)` to 96rem precisely so the old *in-flow* rail was added beside the content rather than taken out of it. Those tiers are still there, because with the script blocked they describe the layout that must not change; `body.has-ad-rail` overrides them (72.75rem for editors, `calc(96rem - var(--ad-rail-w))` for the homepage). Editing panes measure 546px at 1920px and 546px at 1440px, against 546px and 544px before -- no pane is narrower anywhere. The homepage feed comes out a uniform 8px wider, which is the gap/gutter difference. **The homepage's 96rem cap and its rail landed in the same commit, so the cap always had the rail inside it** -- that is why leaving it alone would have grown the cards by a third on a wide screen rather than leaving them alone.
  - **`max-height: calc(100vh - 7rem)` is gone, not merely retuned.** `top: 0` with `bottom: 0` pins the height to the viewport, and the 7rem it used to shed was the header clearance a column starting at y=0 does not need. The internal scroll, the hidden scrollbars and the `:empty` slot collapse all stay.
  - **Verified against a pristine `git archive HEAD` copy served side by side**: with `js/ads.js` blocked, 455 measured properties across five pages and seven widths came back with zero differences. That is the check that matters most here, because the whole placement is supposed to be invisible when it does not fill.
- **Homepage feed reworked: denser ladder, cards as links, hover previews, no descriptions (August 10, 2026).** Five changes at the owner's request, confined to `index.html` and `css/style.css` with no JavaScript touched. Full write-up and every measurement: `docs/implementation/HOMEPAGE_FEED_REWORK.md`. What a future session most needs to know:
  - **Feed previews fill their tile edge to edge.** `.card-preview` used to carry a `--space-md` inset, so each CSS miniature floated inside its tile with its own border reading as a second rectangle inside the card's border, while the one photographic card filled its tile completely. The padding and the miniature's border are gone for feed cards only. **Scoped to `.catalog-grid` on purpose** -- the nine `*-template.html` landing pages use `.card-preview` for a hero preview where the inset is right. The poster miniature keeps its border because there the border is the drawn picture frame, and the `:not(.poster)` guard is load-bearing: without it that selector ties `.mock-doc.poster` on specificity and source order alone would delete the frame.
  - **Catalogue cards are ROUNDED, and that contradicts the theme on purpose (August 10, 2026).** `--radius-card: 0.75rem` on `.catalog-grid .card-preview`, at the owner's instruction, for the Pinterest-style look. **This is the first rounded geometry on the site.** `css/style.css`'s own header said "Geometry: Flat, sharp, rectangular", `PRD.md` line 31 said the same, and `MOCKUP_GENERATOR_IMPLEMENTATION.md` quoted the header to justify the flat-vector product illustrations -- all three now record the exception and its scope, because stale documentation is what the next session trusts over the CSS. CLAUDE.md carries no geometry claim (checked, not assumed). The scope is deliberately narrow: everything outside `.catalog-grid` keeps square corners, and no gradients or drop shadows were introduced anywhere. **If a second rounded surface ever appears, the exception has become drift** and the wording should be rewritten rather than extended. The rounding is load-bearing with `overflow: hidden` on the same element -- that is what clips the hover zoom and both crossfades inside the curve instead of flashing square corners under a rounded mask.
- **The 5-column band is gone; the column count was the culprit, not the gap (August 10, 2026).** Reported as too much empty space between columns. Measured before changing anything: at 1920px the four gaps came to 96px of a 1164px feed, 8.2%. Deleting the gap ENTIRELY would have taken a card from 213.6px to 232.8px, +9%; dropping the fifth column takes it to 279px, **+31%**. So the fifth column went and the gap came down as a second-order improvement (1.5rem to 1rem desktop, 1rem to 0.75rem mobile). Ladder is now 4 at 64rem, 3 at 48rem, 2 below. **The next lever, if the cards still read small, is the 48-64rem band** -- 3 columns give 229.7px at 768px and two would give 352.5px, which is a large card for a tablet and worth looking at rather than assuming.
- **The category tabs and the mobile header hide on scroll-down (August 10, 2026).** One utility in `js/app.js`, `initScrollDirection()`, which knows nothing about any element: it writes a single `.is-nav-hidden` class onto `<body>` and CSS decides who reacts. Passive listener, one rAF per frame, a 6px jitter floor and an 80px reveal zone at the top of the document. Four things worth keeping:
  - **The hidden state is a transform, never `display: none`.** Both elements are `position: sticky`, and collapsing them would drop their boxes out of flow -- **index.html's ad-rail inset works precisely because `.site-header` stays in normal flow and inherits the body padding**. A transform moves the paint, not the box.
  - **The tabs travel their own height PLUS their sticky offset.** `-100%` alone would park them on top of the header on desktop and leave them visible under a hidden header on mobile. Measured hidden bottom edge: exactly 0px at every width.
  - **Desktop header behaviour is unchanged, deliberately.** There is room for it, and it carries the search field, which is the control a visitor is most likely to want mid-scroll.
  - **Scoped to the homepage** with `body:has(.home-layout)`. The class is written on every page loading `js/app.js`, but the editors do not opt in: they carry their own sticky export bar and fixed ad anchor, and a header moving under those is untested. `:focus-within` un-hides both, so keyboard focus can never be stranded off-screen -- those rules tie on specificity and win on source order, so they must stay declared last.
- **The feed cards zoom on hover (August 10, 2026).** A new `.card-media` wrapper inside each `.card-preview` carries `transform: scale(1.06)`. **The wrapper is not decoration**: the poster cards composite a frame photograph over artwork positioned inside the frame's window, and scaling those separately -- each about its own centre -- slides the artwork out of the opening. One wrapper means one transform-origin and the composite stays registered, and it is also why the zoom cannot desync from either hover crossfade. The clip stays on `.card-preview` and the scale on the child, per the rule that scaling a clipping box scales the window too. Transform only, so it cannot repack the grid; gated behind `@media (hover: hover)`; reduced motion is covered by the global block, which drops the animation but keeps the state change.
- **The feed is CSS Grid, and multi-column must not come back.** Both were tried the same day. Multi-column packs each column independently, so ONE two-line card title pushes every card below it in that column down a line: at 1366px the far-right column's second card sat 21px low, and the same appeared at 1200, 1024, 768 and 414. Reserving two lines of title height fixes that and then **empties the fifth column** -- with sixteen equal-height cards `column-fill: balance` fits them into four columns of four, so [3,3,4,3,3] became [4,4,4,4] and the 5-column band silently became a 4-column one. **The ragged heights were the only reason five columns appeared at all.** Multi-column cannot give aligned rows and five columns at once; Grid gives both and needs no reserved height, because every card in a row shares the row's height. Measured after the switch: zero misaligned rows at all nine test widths and on every category filter. Two bonuses -- reading order now runs across rows rather than down columns, removing a trade-off documented since August 9, and the `.card-category` eyebrow wrapping at 320px is absorbed by row height. `align-items: stretch` and `minmax(0, 1fr)` are both load-bearing; see the CSS comment before touching either.
  - **Masonry was built and then removed the same day, deliberately.** Per-card `.ratio-*` preview ratios gave 8 distinct card heights across a 185px range at 1920px; the owner saw it and asked for it gone, so every preview is back to a single 4/5 and the feed reads as an aligned grid (2 distinct heights across 21px at 1920px). **CSS multi-column is still the layout mechanism** -- only the height variation went, and with it the reason the August 9 rebuild gave for choosing columns over grid in the first place. Two things follow. The residual 21px step is card titles wrapping to one or two lines, not a defect; removing it needs a fixed title height that would clip the longer names. And **reading order still runs DOWN each column rather than across rows**, which is inherent to multi-column and is the one thing a real CSS Grid would have fixed -- keeping the columns was chosen over fixing it. Do not re-add the ratio classes as an oversight: their absence is the decision, and `css/style.css` carries a comment where they used to live saying so.
  - **The column ladder is 5 at 100rem, 4 at 64rem, 3 at 48rem, 2 below.** Counts were verified against the FEED's measured width, never the viewport, because the ad rail takes 184px or 324px out of it from 75rem up. The old `column-count: 1` rule below 26rem is gone: the miniatures were rechecked at 320px in two columns (128.5px cards) and the document wordmarks that identify each template still read, so it was removed rather than quietly kept.
  - **Per-category column counts were asked for and are not buildable in this structure.** The feed is ONE set of CSS columns that the tabs filter in place, so the count is global. Making it per-category means each category becomes its own section with its own column context, the tabs switch sections instead of toggling `.is-hidden`, "All" stops being one continuous feed, and the flat search index splits. That is a change to the feed's model, not a CSS change.
  - **The card title IS the anchor now, and that anchor is load-bearing.** No visible CTA remains, but every card still ships one real `<a href>` with real text, with `data-target` and `data-doc` intact and `.card-link::after` still stretched across the card. **These sixteen anchors are the only crawlable inbound links the four editor pages have** -- the same reason the CTAs were converted from buttons to anchors on July 26. Do not let a later redesign turn them into JavaScript handlers.
  - **`.card-preview` needs `isolation: isolate`, and it is not decoration.** The poster hover's base and overlay carry z-index 2 and 3; with no stacking context on the preview they compete in the CARD's context and paint over `.card-link::after`, so the card looks perfect and refuses clicks across its whole preview. Caught before shipping, but it is exactly the defect that would be reported as "the cards randomly do not work".
  - **The on-site search index shrank, and nothing in the brief raised it.** `initSearch()` indexes `.card-body` text, which included the descriptions. Terms that only ever lived in prose -- `tenant`, `landlord`, `photoshoot` -- now match nothing; titles and category eyebrows still match everything a visitor is likely to type. Left as-is deliberately: the fix would be invisible keyword text, which is the thing the same brief ruled out for SEO.
  - **The card titles are now the only indexable prose on the page.** The H1 is `.sr-only`, and the hero, trust band and keyword strip went on August 9. **If homepage impressions fall in Search Console, this belongs on the list of causes**, alongside those August 9 removals. Nothing was hidden in `.sr-only` or an `aria-label` to compensate, on instruction and correctly.
  - **The poster hover downloads 2.3MB on first hover.** `wood-a4-base.png` is 2.07MB. It is declared only inside the `:hover` rule inside `@media (hover: hover)`, so nothing is fetched until a desktop visitor hovers a poster card and touch devices never fetch it at all -- but it is still 2.3MB on the most-indexed page. A downscaled derivative would end it; that is an added asset, which this pass was told not to create.
  - **All three poster cards hover into the same frame,** because `wood-a4` is the only frame mockup in the repository. No asset paths were invented for frames that do not exist. Two more real frames captured through `tools/mockup-admin.html` is the only honest fix.
- **The ad rail is now WIDER than a feed column at 93rem and up, inverting a documented rule (August 10, 2026).** Flagged rather than resolved, because it is a judgement the owner made once by eye and should make again. The rule, recorded above and in `CLAUDE.md`, is that a 300px unit as wide as a content column "stops reading as a rail and becomes a fourth column of adverts" -- learned from a 1366px laptop, and the reason the 160x600 band exists at all. The new ladder inverts the arithmetic on wide screens: at 1920px the feed's columns are 214px against the stack's 300px creative, and at 1488px they are 257px. **The premise did change underneath the rule**: earlier the same day the rail stopped being an in-flow neighbour of the feed and became a column fixed to the window edge with its own background, which is what separates it from the content now, and beside five dense columns a 300px unit at the window edge still reads as a rail rather than a sixth column. That is an opinion, not a measurement. The 1366px case that produced the original complaint is unaffected -- it sits in the single-unit band, 160px against 262px columns. `tests/verify-layout.js` asserted the old form and failed on it; the assertion was **relaxed to "the ad column takes under a quarter of the window" with the reasoning written into the test, not deleted**. If the wide-screen look is wrong, the fix is the ladder or the band, and the test should be tightened back.
- **A real verification suite exists now: `tests/verify-layout.js` (August 10, 2026).** Written after the discovery that **this document described two test files, `test-home.js` and `verify-site.js`, that were never in the repository.** The guarantees they were credited with were real decisions, and the descriptions were accurate about what should hold — but nothing was checking any of them, and the wording invited every future reader to believe the coverage existed and stop verifying by hand. That is worse than having no tests at all. Every claim of the form "a test asserts X" in this document now points at a check that exists and has been proven to fail when X is broken. Four things worth knowing:
  - **Run it before deploying: `node tests/verify-layout.js` from the repository root.** Nothing runs it automatically; there is no CI. `--quick` runs the static half in under a second, the full run takes about two minutes, exit code 1 on any failure. No dependencies to install — it starts its own `npx serve`, drives a browser already on the machine over the DevTools Protocol, and needs no ad-network access. Full description: `docs/guides/RUNNING_THE_VERIFICATION_SUITE.md`.
  - **Every check was mutation-tested: break the thing, confirm the check catches it, put it back.** This was not ceremony. The selector-hook check originally scanned only class names, passed cleanly, and would have sailed straight past a rename of the `data-filter` attribute — the exact bug it was written for — because the filter handlers key on an attribute, not a class. Nothing but deliberately breaking it revealed that. Likewise the band check originally read "at most one band mounts", which passes when a band silently fails to mount, the failure mode that actually costs money; it had to become an exact count. **An assertion that has never failed is not evidence of anything.**
  - **The launch-flow checks must use trusted input** (`Input.dispatchMouseEvent`), never synthetic `MouseEvent` dispatch. A synthetic event is not a user activation, so the `window.open` in `bindLaunchControls` is popup-blocked and the modified-click checks fail against working code. Two false failures came from this before the cause was found. Same trap for clipboard writes, fullscreen, file pickers and autoplay.
  - **Section 4 compares the working tree against `git archive HEAD` with `js/ads.js` blocked**, which is what makes "a dormant zone changes nothing" an enforced property rather than a hope. A difference there is not automatically a bug — if you deliberately changed a layout, it is supposed to differ — so read the reported values rather than reacting to the count.
- **jsPDF primitives over jspdf-autotable.** The invoice/receipt line-item table, checkbox rows and side-by-side party columns in `js/docs.js` are hand-rolled on `doc.text()`, `doc.rect()` and `doc.line()` rather than adding the autotable plugin. Rationale: it keeps the CDN surface identical to the resume builder's single jsPDF include, and CLAUDE.md mandates the native text API specifically. Do not add autotable to "simplify" the table code without weighing that.
- **~~No ads on index.html, ever.~~ Reversed August 6, 2026.** The original argument, kept here because it is still the reason the *format* is constrained: index.html is the page Google actually indexes (loading.html is noindex,nofollow), so ad clutter there carries real SEO/trust risk the isolated loading-page model does not. What survives of it is that only passive banners in the page's own margin are permitted — **active formats on index.html remain banned outright**. See the ad-inventory note above for the placement as it now stands.
- **Editors carry one passive banner (revised July 27, 2026).** The original rule was "no ads on the editors either, ever". It was reversed on a dwell-time argument: an editing session lasts minutes where a catalog visit lasts seconds, so one passive unit on an editor plausibly out-earns the interstitial per session at a far lower interruption cost. Four mutually exclusive viewport bands (three-slot 300x250 rail at 93rem and above, single 160x600 rail 84-93rem, 728x90 leaderboard 48-84rem, 320x50 anchor below 48rem), never two bands at once and never none, no active format, iframe-isolated only. Each slot in the stack carries its own zone key -- repeating one key is not three placements. The rail gate is 84rem rather than 75rem because below that the page is capped by the viewport instead of its own max-width, so the rail stops using spare margin and starts eating the editing panes — at 1200px it cost each pane 68px against the pre-rail layout. The isolation is what keeps "nothing leaves your device" honest, so **Native Banner and any other page-context format is prohibited on editors**. Full reasoning and rejected alternatives: `docs/implementation/EDITOR_PAGE_AD_PLACEMENT.md`. Four documents were updated to match (`PRD.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `site/privacy.html`, `site/terms.html`, `site/about.html`) — if this is ever reversed again, all of them need revisiting.
- **Homepage rebuilt as a pure feed (August 9, 2026).** The owner asked for a Pinterest-style interface and, on seeing the first pass, rejected it as a grid tweak rather than an interface change: "i want to completely change the homepage interface". The second pass removed the hero, then in a follow-up the visible H1 masthead, the four-item trust band and the "Popular starting points" keyword strip as well. What is left above the fold is the category tabs and the cards. Three consequences worth knowing before anyone reverses this:
  - **The homepage now has no visible heading and almost no body copy.** The H1 survives as `.sr-only` because Rule 2 in `CLAUDE.md` mandates one per document and this is the most-indexed page on the site; the words are identical to what a visible one would carry, so it is a layout decision rather than a cloaking one. The card titles and descriptions are the page's only remaining indexable prose. **If Search Console impressions for the homepage fall after this ships, this is the first thing to look at.**
  - **The six landing pages the keyword strip linked are not orphaned.** Every content page's footer carries all six and they remain in `sitemap.xml`. This was checked before the strip was deleted; check it again before deleting anything else from this page, because the homepage carries no footer of its own.
  - CSS multi-column, not `grid-template-rows: masonry`, which is still not shipped unprefixed. The accepted trade is that reading order runs down each column rather than across rows — fine for a catalogue with no inherent sequence, wrong for anything ranked or chronological.

## Extending TemplateBox: Adding a New Template/Editor

Pattern demonstrated by resume.html/docs.html/poster.html/mockup.html, to follow for a fifth tool:

1. Add a card to the `.catalog-grid` in `index.html`. The CTA must be an **anchor** — `<a class="btn btn-block card-link" href="yourpage.html" data-target="yourkey">` — not a button, or the new editor page will have no crawlable inbound link (see the July 26 decision log). Give the card a `data-category` matching an existing or new filter pill, and a filled `.mock-doc` miniature rather than an empty preview. If the new editor serves several cards, also give each card a `data-doc="variantkey"` and consume it with `TB.takePreset()` (see the docs.html pattern above).
2. Add `yourkey: "yourpage.html"` to `EDITOR_ROUTES` in `js/app.js`, to the `EDITORS` registry beside it (label plus the localStorage key the editor writes, which powers the continue strip and the loading page's template name), **and** to the mirrored inline `ROUTES` table in `loading.html` — this is what keeps the loading-page redirect safe from tampering, on both the normal and the fallback path.
3. Build `yourpage.html` + `js/yourpage.js` following the resume/poster split-pane pattern: `.editor-layout` / `.editor-pane` / `.preview-pane` / `.mobile-tabs` CSS classes are already themed and responsive; `TB.sanitize`, `TB.desanitize`, `TB.storageGet`, `TB.storageSet` from `js/app.js` are the shared utilities to reuse for localStorage binding and XSS-safe rendering. Also include the shared editor furniture: `.breadcrumb`, `.editor-head` with `<p class="save-state" id="save-state">`, the export control inside `.preview-actions`, and a `.related-tools` block. Call `TB.markSaved()` after every `TB.storageSet` so the autosave indicator reflects reality.
4. No ad work needed — the new tool automatically routes through the existing monetized `loading.html` flow.
5. Add SEO meta tags, canonical URL, Twitter Card tags, and `SoftwareApplication` + `BreadcrumbList` + `Organization` JSON-LD following the pattern in resume.html/docs.html. Add the new page to `sitemap.xml` with a `lastmod`, and add it to the appropriate footer column — a landing page goes in "Receipts and Invoices" or "Resumes and Creative", an editor in "Editors". The footer is byte-identical across all 22 pages plus the `FOOTER` constant in `js/admin.js`, so change it by generating the block once from a script and writing it to every copy, not by hand-editing 22 files (see the July 27 decision entry).
6. If the new tool serves a distinct search intent, give it a landing page too, following the shape of `rent-receipt-template.html`: own title/H1/canonical, real body content, `FAQPage` schema, and a CTA into the editor with the right preset.
